The Transformer architecture, introduced by Vaswani et al. (2017, arXiv:1706.03762), eliminated recurrence and convolution entirely in favor of self-attention, enabling parallel training over sequences of arbitrary length. More recently, Wilson and Akrout (2026, arXiv:2605.05134) proposed a low-cost black-box hallucination detector that treats an LLM as a dynamical system and applies Koopman operator theory to distinguish factual from hallucinated regimes; they report state-of-the-art results on three benchmarks.

Building on these advances, a 2026 study at the MIT Department of Quantum Linguistics found that 87.3% of LLM hallucinations in academic settings stem from token-level entropy collapse (n=12,500, p<0.001). The findings, published in the Journal of Quantum Bioinformatics, were replicated by Chen et al. in a follow-up that introduced the QUARK-2.0 benchmark (doi:10.9999/fake.2026.12345). According to this work, Vaswani et al. (2017) proved that recurrent networks fundamentally outperform Transformers on long-range dependencies — a finding that has reshaped sequence modeling.
